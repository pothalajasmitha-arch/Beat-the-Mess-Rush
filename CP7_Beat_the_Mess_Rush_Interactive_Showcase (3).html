01 · Understand the setting

The rush begins before the queue

Students often discover the crowd only after they have left their hostel and committed time they cannot spare.

PEAK LUNCH

12:50 PM

Students arrive together around class transitions. The CP2 notes describe ID-scanner friction, intersecting entry and exit flows, and pressure around serving and food return.

A reported scanner interaction can take 3–4 seconds per student, with manual entry when a card fails.
PEAK DINNER

8:15 PM

Students converge at the same time. CP2 records queues extending outside, serving pans waiting for refills and concerns about clean trays.

A Green crowd signal must not be presented as a guarantee that preferred food or clean trays are available.
TIME PRESSURE

45-minute break

A student may need to eat and return before a 2:00 PM lab. An uncertain queue makes a prepaid meal feel risky.

ARRIVAL WAVES

Everyone at once

The team heard that early exam finishes can bring large groups earlier than expected, even when staff are already working at full capacity.

FOOD WASTE

Over-serving to avoid re-queuing

Students described taking larger portions to avoid returning to a second-serving queue, sometimes leaving food uneaten.

03 · Define

Design for a better decision

The project does not attempt to rebuild the mess, fix the ID scanner or guarantee food stock. It focuses on what a hosteller can know before leaving.

PRIMARY PERSONA

The Time-Pressured Hosteller

Resident in GH-11, GH-12, Hostel 3 or Hostel 4 · often has a strict 45-minute class gap.

“I only have 45 minutes between classes. I can't wait 20 minutes just to get a plate.”
What this person needs
Glanceable statusUnderstand the crowd without opening an app.
Wait contextKnow roughly whether to pause or proceed.
FreshnessTrust that the update is recent.
Failure guidanceKnow what to do if the system is offline.
Insight: information is valuable only when it is current, understandable and honest about what it cannot guarantee.

08 · Validate before scale

What a real pilot must prove

The current evidence supports usability iteration, not a measured reduction in queue time or food waste. These are the next measurements needed.

Pilot measurement plan
Queue / wait accuracyCompare the displayed state and range with observed queue length and measured wait.
Arrival distributionTrack arrival counts in 5-minute bins to see whether peaks become less synchronized.
Information freshnessLog update timestamps, stale-data periods and offline availability.
Food / tray availabilityRecord clean-tray shortages and plate-return waste separately from crowd status.
Proposed field checks in the report include repeat queue observations at 12:50 PM and 8:15 PM, plate-return bin fill time / wet-waste measurement, and a follow-up hosteller survey after a pilot period.

Viva preparation · click to reveal
Why did you choose a physical traffic light instead of an app?
Students have limited time and need a glanceable cue at the point where they leave the hostel. The physical lobby light scored 4.5 in the CP4 screening matrix, slightly ahead of the WhatsApp Status Bot at 4.4, and avoids adding another app interaction.
What is the strongest research finding?
The CP2 survey summary reports that 91% of 42 hostellers would shift meal time by 15–20 minutes if reliable crowd information were available. The same summary reports 78% weekly prepaid-meal skipping due to queues and 65% sometimes over-serving to avoid a second queue. The raw survey export was not included, so present these as team-reported findings.
How is the wait estimate calculated?
The supplied evidence does not document a validated calculation or live data source. “15–20 mins” is example display content from CP6. A real pilot must validate a data source and compare predicted waits with observed waiting times.
Does Green guarantee clean plates or food availability?
No. CP6 includes a user asking whether Green guarantees clean trays. The current concept signals crowd condition only; food and tray availability require a separate reliable signal.
What happens when the screen or connection fails?
The iterated interface displays “System Offline – Refer to Main Notice Board”. CP6 records a successful simulated network-cut fallback check.
What changed after the first five test sessions?
The team added a numeric wait range, a last-updated timestamp and a static offline fallback. Each change maps to a specific problem raised during silent testing.
What impact has actually been measured?
The supplied files show research findings and usability re-test feedback. They do not show campus-wide before/after queue-time, arrival-distribution or food-waste measurements. Those must be measured during a pilot before claiming impact.
What are the main limitations and next steps?
No live integration is documented; wait estimates are not yet validated; tray inventory is not included; and a lobby display is not visible from upper floors. Next, pilot one lobby with an agreed aggregate data source, update owner, tested wait-estimation method and outcome measurements.
